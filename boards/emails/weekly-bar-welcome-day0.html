Illustrative premium on a 1 oz retail sticker vs spot · not a quote to trade

Spot can break a round number. Your bill still includes the packaging.

When spot prints a round figure — $4,000, $4,500, whatever the tape is celebrating — social feeds treat the print as the price of gold. It isn’t. A dealer sticker is spot plus premium: minting, shipping, insurance, the shop’s spread, and whatever scarcity story fits the case. A “cheap” 1 g bar sold to “catch the move” often costs more per gram than waiting for a 1 oz coin when premiums normalize.

Reading a sticker takes one habit: divide the asking price by troy ounces (or grams), subtract spot, and look at the percentage left. That residue is what moved for you. Central-bank buying and ETF flows explain why spot climbed; they do not explain why two nearly identical ounces two counters apart differ by eighty dollars.

Pricegraphy sells no metal. We publish the arithmetic so you can leave the aisle with a number you trust — whether you are stacking ounces or melting a chain your aunt left in a drawer.
